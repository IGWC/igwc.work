IGWC General Assembly Launches “Save our Funding” Petition and ICE Emergency Response Plan

Petition: Stop the cuts, Protect workers rights, Save our degrees!

At the April 5th General Assembly Meeting, IGWC launched a petition by unanimous endorsement from the General Assembly. The petition demands that IU take steps to protect its students, workers, departments, and programs in the face of political attacks on higher education.

Now more than ever it's important to come together to demand security: whether that's job security and stable funding, material financial security in the face of an unstable economy, or political security for international workers and workers generally.

As the petition states:

We demand that the IU administration take steps to ensure adequately-funded work is protected for graduate workers with a reasonable timeline to graduation without placing additional burden on department budgets.


We, the graduate employees of Indiana University, demand commitments to secure funding, including:
    – Guaranteed funding through the end of our degree programs, including for graduate employees currently on NSF, NIH, NEH, FLAS, and other precarious federally funded grants.   
    – Financial security for all departments, programs, and cultural centers, especially those that are made even more vulnerable by the ongoing attacks against DEI initiatives.  
    – An immediate raise of 28% to $31k for minimum SAA stipends, to meet the standard set by the recent raise given to President Pamela Whitten, and a path toward universal 12-month contracts for SAAs with a living wage, which according to the MIT cost of living standard is $43k.

The petition also demands that deans from every school meet with representatives of the IGWC to discuss what steps they are taking to meet these demands and keep their students, especially international students, safe.

ICE Emergency Response Plan

While the IGWC calls on IU to take steps to protect its workers and students, we also need to protect ourselves. The General Assembly on April 5th unanimously endorsed a plan to quickly respond to any ICE activity that harms the IU community, especially our fellow graduate workers.

The plan creates a rapid-response protest within 24 hours of ICE activity to demand the immediate release of the people detained, while also creating a pathway for a possible emergency strike-action pending the approval of a General Assembly vote. This would enable the IGWC to change or escalate to respond to the situation and keeping members safe.

We hope to never use this plan, but we should always be prepared to stand in solidarity with each other—an injury to one is an injury to all.

For more information or to help organize, talk to your Department Organizer or join our Organizing meetings every Tuesday at 5pm at Canterbury House (719 E 7th St) and on Zoom.
