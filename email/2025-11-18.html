Sanctuary Campus Now!

What do we mean by Our University: Part 5

In April, after approving an emergency plan to authorize a strike vote if IU did not protect and support any graduate worker who had their visa unjustly revoked, we successfully negotiated some of the best protections for international graduate workers in the country: IU will not unenroll students due to their Visa/SEVIS status, proactively check the SEVIS database, and work with students who are unjustly impacted to create a flexible pathway to degree.

Crucially, IU also agreed not to proactively provide information to the Department of Homeland Security without a warrant.

Given the illegal and unjustifiable weaponization of ICE and Homeland Security against non-citizens, IU can still do more to ensure a safe and stable campus. It can declare itself a sanctuary campus and refuse to cooperate with federal immigration enforcement agencies. If IU is legally compelled to work with those agencies, it can notify students of the presence of these agencies on campus.

IU can also create an Emergency Research Continuity Fellowship equivalent to the student's contract with IU, as well as continue any student and employee benefits and waive tuition requirements to cover students who are impacted by these revocations.

A Sanctuary Campus and financial support for students impacted by unjust immigration enforcement aren't separable from any of our other demands: a Sanctuary Campus or Emergency Funding without a living wage leaves students in a precarious position as they try to look after themselves and their families. A Sanctuary Campus policy must be negotiated with the input and collaboration of graduate students, faculty, and undergraduate students—collective bargaining and union recognition are vital to producing strong protections and trust.

IU is a better school when the workers have more say over their conditions.

A Sanctuary Campus isn't just about security for IU students in the face of unjust immigration enforcement. It's about creating the conditions that enable quality research and education. A graduate worker or student who doesn't have to worry about their immigration status to see their family or complete their research is going to produce better research. When graduate workers don't have to stress about where the next meal will come from as the cost of living rises while their pay stagnates is going to teach more effectively.

In addition to maintaining enrollment, IU must ensure that international students receive financial support to finish their degrees regardless of their student or visa status.
IU must also refuse to cooperate with federal immigration enforcement and must provide the fullest protection to students of vulnerable populations.

A safer, better IU start with workers standing together.
