Graduate Workers Storm out of Admin Q&A

Last Friday, admin including Chancellor David Reingold sidestepped questions about unionization, low wages, and IU as a Sanctuary Campus

"It's ridiculous how long they talked without saying anything," one graduate worker said after walking out.

Last Friday, in a Q&A hosted by GPSG, Chancellor David Reingold and other administrators offered tepid, non-commital answers in regards to a wide range of issues impacting graduate education at IU—answers that ultimately culminated in grad workers walking out of the meeting altogether.

The first question to Chancellor Reingold asked simply: "Why aren't graduate workers recognized alongside the three other recognized unions at IU?" Under IU's collective bargaining policy (HR 12-20), IU employees may petition for collective bargaining representation, which grad workers have done in 2022, 2024, and currently in our card campaign. Yet, IU admin have refused to follow this policy, despite GPSG passing a resolution explicitly affirming grad workers' collective bargaining rights.

Reingold answered by saying recognition and other union demands were not the decision of the admin before taking a different angle: "Is there actual evidence, um, you know, at Indiana, on this campus that unionization does actually tranlate into the sorts of things, uh, into the sorts of things that are being advanced and advocated for, and I think an honest sort of analysis of that, uh, in terms of observing the campus either here or elsewhere would suggest that in fact those benefits are limited."

By "the sorts of things that are being advanced and advocated for," Reingold is of course referring to grad workers' demand for a living wage (graduate worker wages are 45% below the living wage for Bloomington), secured funding for those programs impact by funding cuts, as well as protections against ICE by designating IU as a Sanctuary Campus.

Since the Chancellor only just arrived at IU, he might not remember that graduate worker unionization efforts and strikes resulted in a 46% raise for graduate workers—the largest raise in over a decade at IU. He might not be aware that unionizing graduate workers forced IU to eliminate over a thousand dollars of fees paid back to the university, or that our organizing in 2025 ensured some of the most robust protections for international students facing unjust threats from the federal government.

When a graduate worker pointed out that the best-paid graduate workers in IU's peer institutions in Big 10 all had graduate worker unions, the Chancellor interrupted the Q&A to say that Indiana was too different from those universities to make a comparison. In short, the Chancellor essentially argued that IU could not recognize a grad workers union because grad worker wages were too low. Go figure!

Reingold, when asked what the living wage in Bloomington was, answered correctly that it is around 40k (it's $43,368 to be exact). When he was asked how much a graduate worker makes, he could not say.

We've taken the liberty of drawing up a chart for the Chancellor, showing graduate worker's wages compared to the living wage (which is 45% more than graduate workers make!) and the Chancellor's wages.

When he was asked how many hours a graduate student works, he confidently answered 20. Does any graduate student at IU work 20 hours a week? Between our paid work, research, grants, coursework, and applications—all of which IU implicitly and explicitly profits from, either directly in the case of grants and research or indirectly in state and federal subsidies for coursework—graduate workers consistently work far more than 20 hours a week.

The Chancellor dismissed concerns about ICE in general without elaborating what admin were doing to ensure campus remained safe, and never directly answered why IU shouldn't be a sanctuary campus—a simple way to ensure graduate workers could be safe at IU. After the Chancellor and other administrators gave increasingly non-commital answers about complying with the law and the "complex legal landscape" regarding ICE, department/program mergers, and unionization, graduate workers stood up and left the room.

"What a waste of time. I couldn't get a straight answer to any of my questions," said another graduate worker. "If they won’t even listen to the concerns from student government, clearly the 'proper channels' aren't enough to make sure we are heard."

What will get us heard? Signing union cards, standing in solidarity with each other, and building real leverage over admin so they can't talk in circles.
